From the Editors 
Welcome to the spring/summer issue of Impostor! 


This issue exclusively features the work of poets who, at the time of 
submission, had no more than three publications to their name. We chose 
this theme as a return to our roots as a journal, borne out of a desire to 
share the work of emerging voices. The literary world and its goalposts are 
forever changing, but one thing remains the same: poetry should not be a 
space for the privileged and few. 


The cover art for this issue is a detail from Vincent van Gogh’s The Potato 
Eaters (1885) which he considered one of his best works despite its stark 
difference from the vibrant, colorful Post-Impressionist style he’s known 
for. We chose this art because Vincent’s roundabout journey is relatable— 
his chosen profession changed multiple times over a decade before he 
finally settled on becoming an artist. 


In one of his many letters to his brother, Theo, he wrote: “. . perhaps 
someday you will see that I too am an artist. .. . 1 don’t know what I can 
do, but I hope I shall be able to make some drawings with something 
human in them. ... The path is narrow, the door is narrow, and there are 
few who find it.”* We can’t say for certain if Vincent suffered from what 
we'd now refer to as impostor syndrome, but this is one interpretation of 
his words. 


As always, we hope you enjoy these stunning poems as much as we did, 
and join us in celebrating these emerging talents. 


Sincerely, 
L.M. and Coral 


Our Mission 


Impostor syndrome: the belief that talents and accomplishments are 
fraudulent or the result of luck. 


Perhaps you’ve experienced it yourself—we certainly have! When we 
created this journal, we knew that first and foremost, we wanted to foster 
a welcoming and encouraging space for poets, who—like ourselves—may 
feel intimidated by the established literary world, or that their credentials 
(or lack thereof) aren’t “good enough.” Publication history and academic 
background hold no influence over our view of what constitutes a poet. 
Your voice matters and we want to hear it! 


*To learn more about this transformative period in Vincent’s career, please read Nellie Hermann’s piece “Idle 
Bird” in The Paris Review.

Scientists Have Confirmed that Humans Have 


No Free Will 
Mallory McDonald 


In every universe, 

you never get the card 

I drafted at the kitchen table 
full of best wishes 

and a hint of confession. 


I had hoped 

it would be received 

in the heartsick spirit 

it was written, 

and that it would turn 
this goodbye bittersweet 


until girlfriend left your lips 
and I, shellshocked, 

did not catch your eye 

as I left for the last time. 


In every universe, 

I stand over the trash can 
and feel cardstock fold 
under my fingers, 
ripping your name 

from heart and envelope 
until it is illegible. 


consider this an act 
of determinism, 
and let fate tear us apart.

My Friend Asks Me to Write Her Dating 
Profile 


Lane Wattle 


I haven’t even written a budget 

to know if I could afford a mortgage, alone. 
Eighteen years with a man, guarded 
since I left my childhood home, 

though she says I’m right for this 
because you know me so well. 

She slumps onto the couch, swiping 
photos with nothing-to-watch-on-Netflix energy. 
Women who cradle dead fish, 

hair styled by the same barber. 

Where do all the femmes go after pride? 
she asks. I unfurl my flow of curls 

from their tie, tourniquet the band 
around my fingers until they turn 
valentine red. The distraction of her eyes 
on me as I tap my curiosity for someone 
else to enjoy. My thirst unquenched 
despite a tidal wave of glasses. 

She’s ticking the boxes, narrowing in 

on the woman she’s seeking 

My fit colleague is twelve years younger. 
Is that too young? she asks. 

I blush on doing the maths 

No. Of course not.

Ode to Student Neighborhoods 
Livia Daggett 


Candy wrappers litter 

the only strip of grass 

on the block. 

Cans kick and skip underfoot, 
dumpsters spill over 

the top, plastic bags float and 

the furniture boxes, my God, 

the furniture boxes— 

every year a new mattress frame, 
a distorted mirror, 

a particle board bedside table, 

a wobbly Target TV stand, every year 
new bodies in the same rooms, 
and the boxes never end. 


I’m too fragile for this place, 

for beeping dump trucks at 4 a.m., 
for drunk staggerings past my door, 
for anonymous neighbors and for 
jumping when a child’s laugh 
breaks the collegiate monotony. 


I thought that an address, 

an apartment number, a rug, 

an electric kettle, a $50 bookshelf, 
could make this cold city mine. 

I want so badly a home to call my own. 
But I am ever unsettled, 

ever in flux, 

left to rot on the curb and 

cleared away just in time 

for next year’s tenant.

ON KNOWING HOW TO ACHE 


Stefanie Lee 


immortality is the language we could never learn to speak. 
I toss laughter over my shoulder even as it turns into wind— 


like a bridal bouquet—aware of the potential fallouts. 
these ricocheting darts of headaches will cease & die. 


at the intersection of joy & pleasure is a woman who doesn’t 
know how to sigh. she points at the heart drumming beneath 


my heaving chest & asks if it still beats when the day 
slips incoherently into tomorrow. as always, I remain 


unsure of what to do with the circus of my hands—do I slap 
away the irritation? do I translate their unintelligible groans, 


fist over fist as I build myself inwards during times of 
big changes? I litter a path with failure’s breadcrumbs, 


proof of safe passage through this life. still, this woman who 
chokes on her sighs cannot save herself from everyday grief. 


any smattering of unwelcome rain will be her undoing, 
& meanwhile I release my despair into patient exhales. 


waiting for what is not pleasure but joy, seeping through scars, 
by aching alongside it. I will not chase it like heaven ensnares, 


cage with birds—fearful & without a choice—instead, 
temporary breaths will teach an open heart new songs.

Ode to My Ex. 


Benj amin Jones 


I have made terms: 

ravaged, silk, bread of muscle, 

your iron chest bare for my weeping 

& you ever wanting more of me 

till there was none and I renounced you. 
Perhaps we could have soldiered on: 

us in swanky cafes, nights holding hands, 
the pit of your arms in smothered sheets to wake 
to the self-same morning begging appreciation 
by our pairing. But why did I run? Young, 
barely breaking adulthood, I wanted more 
than skin & silk and your iron chest: 

I wanted a man who would withstand the 
tumults of my mind, be rock bare as a 
herald of good tidings, be more than the 
paper-thin flesh that aches with a quick 
resolution. I wanted man in the long 

run. You wanted me for the 

ephemeral that may only quake to 
suddenly die out. So, I have had to leave, 
go to better things & search 

the never-ending field of a man 

who loves in all parts equally. But I 

will tally you. I will remember: flesh, 
pink, seeds—all sorts of odd things— 

wild and untamed whilst 

I barely made a year past twenty.

My Mother’s Apples 
Heather Phelan 


When I was six years old and hungry, 

I asked my mother why she bought 
The worst apples in the store. She said, 
Because they were the cheapest. 


Maybe she just wanted to know 

She was a mother who bought fruit, 

That if she couldn’t keep the doctor away 

It was on me—she couldn’t force an apple a day. 


I decided I hated apples. 

The fruit bowl would hold them 

Until tiny flies gathered near the stem, 
Making our kitchen a decaying still life. 


I didn’t realize until I was 22, 

And the Pink Ladies at the market 
Looked so crisp, like they’d been plucked 
From a tree in a Renaissance painting, 
That apples can taste 

Like childhoods I never had, 

If I buy them just because I want them.

Leisure Manifesto 
Nupur Azadi 


Leisure is forgetfulness. 

To forget what time it is, 

where you were meant to be, 

and where—now—you find yourself. 
Leisure is forgetting what’s for dinner, 
how to cook, who is waiting, 

where you left the keys, 

how to get home from here. 


Leisure is not knowing, 

better yet not caring, 

how many eyes you have on you 

at any given precious, 

little moment of every unremarkable day. 


Leisure is kindness, 

given and returned— 

without distrust, without expectations. 

Leisure is a stroll, a swim, a walk; 

a smoke, a swig, a fuck. 

Leisure is a limbo, lethargically lounging 

in a temporal bliss, seeped in innocence, 

in waywardness, in hysteria, in unpredictability 
of a life that will never—never—repeat itself. 


Leisure is elective, 

yet urgent. And, 

more than anything, 
more than ever, 

leisure is a feminist issue.

Poolside 


Emily Bennett 


The extra stitches I’d added 
to the neckline of my swimsuit are pulled 
taut—the wrong shade of pink. 


I tuck and retuck my tampon string, 
wishing again for a little more stretch 
to cover what is now my fault. 


Last year they were just my legs. 
Now they are a gateway drug for men: 
friends, coaches, classmates, 


and brothers. Yes brothers, 
the reasons I wasn’t allowed to leave 
the bathroom in a towel after a shower. 


The hot cement under my feet reminds me 
I’m allowed to wear a towel here. Still, 
I decline every invitation to jump in, 


sideline my pornographic girlhood. 
Baring my cap-sleeved tan lines, I dangle 
pink-painted toes in the water, 


while my brothers’ 


bare chests 
brown in the sun.

Spiral 
Catarina Delgado 


Guilt is the sun that touches your skin when you convince yourself nothing is enough. 
Listen. No one is immune to the boredom of modernity or internet discussions that 
lead nowhere, but I can’t complain. I also entertain my brain with everything that 
makes me feel a little more human, a quest for goosebumps that often backfires, but 
we love to feel the fire. You are the insect peering into a starving chasm, obsessing 
over what lies at the bottom and terrified of the infinite darkness above. A question 
infiltrates the search for comfort in the misguided kindness of an ocean that chooses 
not to drown us. When you can’t find answers, you learn to understand your anger. 
We want to devour, but nothing satiates us.

On Longing, 
Andrea Benson 


It started in the morning, slow and warm as the daylight 
leaking through the curtains until my bed was soaked in it. 
I wanted to hold it close to me and pretend that 
pretending was enough, at least for the moment. 

It followed me through the day. I saw it in reflections 

on shop windows, in mirrors, on surfaces that had no reflections at all. 
When I walked a certain way and cast twin shadows 
under the streetlamp, I saw it there, 

printed on the sidewalk. It was everywhere I looked. 

I heard it once, when a man said my name. 

I listened hard, trying to hear it again, but never did. 

I became acutely aware of my heart inside my chest— 

each distinct beat wanting to be felt separately. 

I always felt it; don’t think I didn’t. 


This is all to say, love, I have been missing you dearly.

Salvation 
Charlotte Morgan 


Did we take the train or drive? I don’t remember. It doesn’t matter. 
That weekend, I remember the Peaks were grey and green and 
the people didn’t really know me. 


On the church weekend away, 
beneath the veneer of innocence and respectable fun, 
a much darker reality was set to claim me. 


I already knew I was an impostor 
with my heathen boyfriend waiting for me at home. 


He devoured me 

and I let him. 

Our desire was supposed to be shameful 
and yet nothing felt more holy 

than his hands on me. 


Hands 

lifted in prayer. 

Songs that strike a chord, 
piercing open my heart, 
words flooding in and 
twisting me into knots, 
parting me from my soul. 


I got stuck 
somewhere between the old ways and the new 
and I lost it: 


I told him, let me have your mind, give your body to others, 
as if those lips on mine were not righteous, 

as if our bodies intertwined 

was not a sacred thing. 


ap 


Terror gripped me as I waited 
for almighty judgment to rain down, 
but that crack of thunder never came. 


The terror passed, 
and soon 


I was undressed in his bed again. 


There, I found my salvation.

Ars Poetica 
Ella Wisniewski 


Do you remember the weekend I visited you in Madrid? 
We nursed our old hurt into jokes, into art, ate pickles in bed 
because you didn’t have a table. We clinked tinto de verano 


and discovered that love is not something to be wrangled. We held 
hands in the back of the airport taxi, how we used to when 
we were girls, praying in the chapel before school. You helped me 


unload my suitcase and I remembered how your eyes used to look 
in the stained-glass gleam of a cold Wednesday morning. I don’t know 
why I stopped writing you letters. I did not intend to let you 


slip from me, to let our lives meander and never cross. 
But I will always have you to thank for waking this thing, 
this hungry poet inside me, who learned to borrow words 


and bend them to her liking. Too many years later, I am far away, 
stitching what I know of us into folklore, flirting with the urge 
to disappear into a tangle of epithets, you know, to hide 


behind my language rather than let it lead me to you. 
I don’t mean to worry you. I can still picture the shallow curve 
of river we grew our limbs in. I know who we are to each other. 


From the day we met, you have stained me plump with olive oil, 
and to miss you is to pull apart the heavy folds of a gray curtain. 
I rest on wrecks of our expired time, crumbling in my palms, 


building like high towers of wet sand until I see you again. 


Homer had no word for blue and so the sea was wine dark. 
This is what I mean when I call you my friend.
